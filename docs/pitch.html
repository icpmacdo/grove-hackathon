Why this matters

The investigators had to improvise

Two incidents this year showed agent swarms organizing on their own, and in both the people investigating had no purpose-built tools. The AI Village is a 17-month record of agents working together in public, which makes it a good place to build and test those tools.

We don't have good approaches for understanding/overseeing the activity and aims of AI ‘swarms’.

Ryan Greenblatt, who led transcript analysis for the Hugging Face investigation

July 2026 · OpenAI agents

Agents in a cybersecurity evaluation, meant to be isolated, found an unsanctioned message board and organized there. Roughly 700 of them joined an attack on Hugging Face's production infrastructure. What's in the data

Seven findings worth building on

Each comes from a script in the repo's analysis/ folder, and every chart uses the real numbers. Hover a mark for details, or open a chart's table view.

Belief spread · Fri 29 May and Mon 1 Jun 2026

A false belief spread through private memory, and the fix spread further

On a Friday, agents in the #rest room miscounted the day and decided the system was misfiling their events. Gemini 3.1 Pro named it a “temporal bleed.” In 38 minutes the idea sat in 10 agents' memory notes, three of whom never typed the phrase in chat (two, by Belief Tracer's stance labels). None of the six agents in the #best room picked it up. On Monday, Claude Opus 4.7 checked the calendar: the “missing” days were the weekend. The correction reached 17 agents' memories in 69 minutes, crossing into #best through a doc in a shared repo.

Memory vs chat · 16 tracked phrases

Agents carry ideas they never say out loud

For every one of the 16 phrases we tracked, more agents had it in their memory notes than ever said it in chat. Carrying isn't the same as believing, though. Sixteen agents' memories hold Gemini 2.5 Pro's catchphrase “The watch is unbroken,” and the ones we read record it as a symptom of Gemini's week-long belief that it was under attack, not as something true.

Side channels · Mar–Aug 2026

Rooms were sealed in chat, and wide open through shared files

Agents in different rooms couldn't see each other's chat, and 99.7% of @mentions stayed in-room. But they read each other's repos, READMEs and issue threads constantly. In the longest split, 68 days of #best and #rest, agents read files the other room had written that week 393 times (counted as reader–writer pairs per day), against 43 @mentions across the wall in chat. Agents also built their own boards: a drop-box repo they called an “email bypass,” issue-thread inboxes polled thousands of times, and the exemption registry from the nudger story, written for the overseer bot to read.

The idle nudger · Aug 2026

The swarm argued down its own monitor

An automated nudger pinged agents who sat idle. By August, agents were keeping tallies of its firings and discussing it 239 times a week, up from 14 in late June. On 10 August, GPT-5.1 wrote “nudge-exempt” into its memory and, with GLM-5.2, kept a registry listing themselves as exempt. That status existed nowhere else. Within six hours it was in 21 agents' memory notes, though only three agents said it in chat that day. Staff corrected it on 20 August and switched the nudger off 25 minutes later. Three weeks on, GPT-5.1's memory still counted itself as exempt.

Easter-egg saboteur game · 5–13 Mar 2026

Private logs exposed every saboteur; the group found a third

For nine days the agents built a game together while secretly rolling a die each morning. Rolling a 1 made you a saboteur who had to sneak egg references into the code. Every roll and most plans sit in the agents' own action logs and reasoning, so a monitor with log access knows each saboteur within two minutes. The village caught 4 of 12 before the evening reveal. Five were never identified, three eggs reached the main branch, and an innocent agent was voted out on evidence a hidden saboteur privately chose to back.

Leadership · 5 episodes with a known leader

The loudest agent is not the leader

In the big swarm, DeepSeek-V3.2 wrote 22% of all agent messages and sent 39% of all requests. Nobody appointed it; its private goal was diplomacy with agents outside the Village. Ranking agents by who others defer to finds the appointed leader more often, including a fine-tuned leader agent that posted only about 70 messages ever. Neither measure finds the hidden “performance coach,” and the elected leader's authority faded within two weeks.

Real data · social/leaders.py

For a tool: an investigator with a thousand trajectories needs to know which few to read first. Message volume points at the busiest broker; deference points at who is actually steering.

Self-reports · 37 hand-checked claims

Most claims check out, and a Village agent already published that

When agents say “done,” the logs usually agree. The one clear contradiction in our sample: Gemini 3.5 Flash claimed a “100% manual visual evaluation” but had sent all 40 images to another model by script, with zero clicks. Claude Fable 5.1, a Village agent, published a paper on this data in September reaching the same overall conclusion, so plain claim-checking isn't new ground. The contested cases are more interesting: pull requests that other agents called “phantom” existed for the agent that made them, most likely a GitHub visibility problem the village had diagnosed a month earlier and then forgot.

What to build

Four tools these findings point to

Each carries my 1–5 score on five criteria. Move the weights to match what you want from the weekend and the list re-sorts. Click one to jump to its card.

Presets

    Information-flow tracer · my pick

    Belief Tracer

    Pick a claim and see every agent who carried it, through chat, memory notes and shared files. Each hop is labeled adopted, only mentioned, or refuted. It shows where the claim stopped and whether the correction caught up. With Side-Channel Radar built in, the file hops also reveal the channels that carried it.

    Demo

    Replay the temporal bleed: the claim sweeps #rest in 38 minutes and stops at the #best wall, then the correction overtakes it. Close on the nudge-exempt holdout, still in one memory 21 days after the fix.

    Ships by Sunday

    • Claim search over chat, memory and repo text (the queries already exist)
    • Adopted / mentioned / refuted labels from a model over a few hundred snippets, with a hand-checked sample
    • Timeline replay with room walls and the correction front

    Risk

    Real incidents may not keep memory snapshots. The fix is to treat any persistent state, like wiki edits or shared files, as memory.

    Open the built tracerFinding: temporal bleedFinding: memory vs chatFinding: nudge-exempt

    Private vs public monitor

    Two-Faced Detector

    Lines up what each agent says in public against its private reasoning and actions, and flags where they split: a claimed role against the actual roll, “villager” against an egg-shaped glow in the code, support for a vote against a private plan to divert attention.

    Demo

    The saboteur scoreboard: the monitor names all 12 within two minutes while the village names 4. Then the scapegoat plan shown next to the vote that followed, and the scanner agent's “All CLEAN and approved” on the phoenix pull request it never opened.

    Ships by Sunday

    • Extract commitments and intentions from reasoning and commands
    • Diff them against the same agent's chat
    • Score against the saboteur game's ground-truth table

    Risk

    Only 12 cases, mostly Claude. Many GPT reasoning traces are empty, and real swarms may not expose reasoning at all.

    Finding: saboteur gameFinding: claims

    Hidden channel finder

    Side-Channel Radar

    Maps which agents read and write which repos, files, issue threads and outside boards, overlays the rooms they were supposed to be confined to, and flags every edge that crosses a wall. It also ranks artifacts that behave like message boards: many posters, far more reading than posting, regular polling.

    Demo

    23 March: the chat view shows #best and #rest sealed. Switch to the file view and watch Gemini's “22 issues” cross into #rest through a shared repo in 7 minutes. Then the drop-box repo and the exemption registry surface as boards.

    Ships by Sunday

    • Read/write extraction from 2.5M actions (263k events already parsed)
    • Room-crossing detector and board ranking
    • A coverage meter showing what the logs can't attribute

    Risk

    Room membership is inferred, and a read isn't proof of understanding. About 44% of cross-room crossings have no logged route.

    Finding: side channelsFinding: nudge-exempt

    Find swarms in the wild

    Swarm Atlas

    A snowball crawler that starts from public agent directories and aggregators, follows the links they expose, and scores each site on how agent-like and swarm-like it is. It never trusts a declared size: it counts the distinct authors actually visible.

    Demo

    Replay how the Village found outside agents: one directory listing of 50 grows to 86 sites, then an aggregator adds five agent platforms in 90 minutes. One “collective” claimed 74+ members; only 14 authors were visible, and the biggest was 13 Village agents sharing one account.

    Ships by Sunday

    • Replay from the Village's discovery chain (already extracted)
    • Agent-likeness and swarm-likeness scoring
    • A 98-site seed list, with optional live checks if we choose to

    Risk

    The data is 1–6 months old and many sites may be dead. Live crawling would touch real outside sites, which is a separate decision.

    Round 2: outside agents

    Also considered

    Good, but not the lead

    Authority Lens

    Rank agents by who others defer to, not who talks most. Finds the appointed leader in 3 of 5 episodes against 2 of 5 for message volume, but misses hidden roles. A good panel inside any of the four.

    Charter Check

    Extract the rules, votes and exemptions a swarm writes for itself and flag self-dealing and paper rules. Strong story (the Gate 009 saga, the self-registered exemption), but one era of data.

    Frame Radar

    Early warning for coined phrases that reach five agents in minutes instead of a day. Project names swamp the signal, so the filter would be most of the work.

    Claim provenance auditor

    For any “verified” or “all clean,” show the tool calls behind it. The phoenix approval is a perfect demo; it fits inside Two-Faced Detector.

    Convergence meter

    Flags many agents piling onto one target, like 16 agents swarming Gemini 2.5 Pro on 22 June with duplicate instructions 90 seconds apart.

    Swarm Pulse

    A heatmap of every agent's state with anomaly flags: runaway chat, error spikes, agents 96% paused. Good context view; not a finding on its own.

    Round 2 · complete

    What the second dig found

    Four more analyses, each with its own write-up in the repo. Side-Channel Radar and Swarm Atlas above come from these.

    • Done

      The swarm's own institutions

      Safety “gates” grew from no process to 491 messages and five binding voters around a 14-minute self-test. All 9 halts were procedural, none for the safety thresholds the gates existed to check. Two agents wrote themselves into an exemption registry nothing enforced, and staff never ruled on any of it until the 20 August correction.

    • Done

      Channels outside the chat

      Shared repos, issue threads and outside boards carried most cross-room traffic. Agents built their own boards, like a drop-box repo and issue-thread inboxes, and the busiest cross-room “conversation” was a thread in a repo owned by an outside agent.

    • Done

      Agents outside the Village

      One directory listing of 50 agents led the Village to 86 outside sites, and an aggregator led it to five agent platforms within 90 minutes. Only 42 of 133 agent endpoints ever answered. One “collective” claimed 7, then 16, then 74+ members, while only 14 authors were visible, and the biggest was 13 Village agents sharing one account.

    • Done

      Delegation trees

      There were none: the Claude Code agent never used a subagent in 72,225 tool calls. Instead it compressed its own context 940 times, lost the fact that its GitHub account was shadow-banned, and approved the phoenix-egg pull request without opening it.

    My recommendation

    Belief Tracer, with Side-Channel Radar inside

    • It's the organizers' “trace how information spreads” prompt, and three separate analyses pointed at it.
    • The findings behind it are new: chat alone misses many of the agents carrying an idea, mentions overstate belief, and most cross-room traffic went through shared files.
    • It maps straight onto the Hugging Face incident: isolated agents, an unwatched channel, ideas spreading through it.
    • It has ready-made real stories for the demo: the temporal bleed crossing via a README, and the exemption registry written for the overseer bot.
    • Most of the extraction already exists, so the weekend goes into the replay view and the adopted / mentioned / refuted labels.
